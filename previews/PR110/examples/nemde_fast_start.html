Fast-start dispatch ​

The NEM has no central unit commitment. A participant decides for itself when to start a slow plant, and offers it into dispatch once it is running. The exception is the fast-start unit: a generator, typically a gas turbine or a hydro unit, that can synchronise and reach its minimum load within minutes. Such a unit can ask NEMDE to start it when the price justifies it. In return NEMDE must respect the shape of its start-up, which the unit declares as a dispatch inflexibility profile.

This chapter explains the profile, the constraints it adds to dispatch, and the two solves NEMDE uses to decide which units to start. It follows AEMO's Fast Start Unit Inflexibility Profile. Symbols follow the notation table.

The inflexibility profile ​

A profile has a minimum load in MW and four durations in minutes, submitted with the unit's daily energy offer.

ParameterMeaning
Time to synchronise after the instruction to start. Output is zero
Time to ramp from zero to minimum load
Time the unit must stay at or above minimum load
Time to shut down from minimum load

The four durations are non-negative, at least one is positive, and they sum to less than 60 minutes. A unit that offers all four as zero is dispatched as an ordinary unit.

The position of a unit on its profile is its mode, together with the minutes it has spent in that mode. NEMDE receives both at the start of each interval.

ModeAEMO's nameLastsOutput at the end of the interval

In modes 1 and 2 the output is fixed: the unit follows its start-up and cannot be dispatched up or down. In modes 3 and 4 the profile is only a floor, and the unit can be dispatched anywhere above it. The floor in mode 4 falls from to zero over minutes, which lets the unit shut down no faster than it declared. A segment with zero duration is skipped.

The first constraint is the floor. The second, present only while the output is fixed, turns the floor into an equality. Both slacks have a penalty factor of 1130, item 10 of AEMO's Schedule of Constraint Violation Penalty Factors, v8.0. That is below the ramp-rate factor of 1155 and above the availability factor of 370, so a unit on its profile is held there even above its declared availability.

Deciding which units to start: two solves ​

A start is a yes-or-no decision, which a linear programme cannot take. If the profiles were simply enforced, NEMDE could not tell whether starting a unit is worthwhile. AEMO resolves this with two solves in every interval.

  1. First solve, without profiles. All profile constraints are ignored. Units that start the interval in modes 0, 1 or 2 are also freed from their energy ramp rates and from the joint energy and FCAS ramping constraints. The solve shows how much each fast-start unit would produce if it were fully flexible.

  2. Commitment. Each unit's mode at the end of the interval is set from its mode at the start and its first-solve result.

    • An offline unit is started if its first-solve target is positive. It then moves along its profile; if   is under five minutes it is already in mode 3 by the end of the interval.

    • A unit already on its profile continues along it.

    • A unit that has completed runs as an ordinary unit until its target reaches zero.

  3. Second solve, with profiles. The profile constraints are enforced for the modes found in step 2. This solve produces the published targets and prices.

In mode 4, before has elapsed, the unit's lower limit is the tighter of its ramp-down limit and the falling profile floor. Units that begin the interval in modes 0, 1 or 2 have no joint energy and FCAS ramping constraints in either solve (FCAS Model in NEMDE, §6.5, p. 28).

Correcting the starting mode ​

Before the first solve AEMO corrects the starting mode in two cases, using the unit's metered output .

The unit is on its profile and is now offered unavailableAvailability is zeroMode 0 if it was in mode 0 and is below 1 MW; otherwise mode 4 with completed

In both cases, mode 4 with completed means the unit is treated as an ordinary running unit that can be ramped down and shut down.

How the run implements it ​

nem_dispatch_sequence builds the run of each interval as two PowerSimulations decision models that run in order at the same timestamp, when the System has an available fast-start unit (a unit whose energy offer has a positive to ):

  1. Commitment run. The full dispatch without profile rows. A fast-start unit that starts the interval in mode 0, 1 or 2 has no energy ramp rows and no joint ramping rows.

  2. End state. From the commitment run's target, fast_start_end_state commits a unit starting in mode 0 when that target is above 0 MW, then advances it through modes 1, 2 and 3 by the five minutes of the interval. A unit never leaves mode 4 in the run.

  3. Dispatch run. The same dispatch with the profile rows of the end mode, elastic at the fast-start penalty factor of 1130 times the Market Price Cap (FAST_START_CVP_FACTOR). Its targets, prices and violations are the run's results.

In the dispatch run a unit ending in mode 0, 1 or 2 has no energy ramp rows. A unit that left mode 2 during the interval ramps up from its minimum load instead of its starting output,   , where is the time since the end of mode 2 and the lesser of the offered and telemetered ramp-up rates per minute. Every other unit keeps its usual ramp rows. Participant fixed loadings are dropped while the unit starts the interval in mode 1 or 2.

The starting mode and minutes in mode come from the previous interval's DISPATCHMODE and DISPATCHMODETIME in DISPATCHLOAD, the minutes capped at the length of the mode. The profile and come from the version of the unit's energy offer in force at the interval: the BIDDAYOFFER row whose OFFERDATE equals the LASTCHANGED of the interval's BIDPEROFFER_D row, so a rebid during the day changes them. Pass fast_start = false to nem_dispatch_sequence, or --fast-start false to the batch runner, to solve one model per interval, without profiles.

Deviation in AustralianElectricityMarketsSimulations

AEMO: corrects the starting mode before the first solve, follows the profile from the end state of the previous interval, and applies the profile to every fast-start unit. AustralianElectricityMarketsSimulations:

  • does not model the corrections of the starting mode above, so a unit that NEMDE returns to mode 0 starts from its published mode;

  • in a closed-loop run, still starts each unit from AEMO's observed mode and minutes in mode, not from the end state of the previous solve;

  • gives batteries no profile rows; a battery with a profile keeps its usual rows;

  • decides the regulation joint ramping exemption from the starting mode in both runs; see FCAS joint ramping.
