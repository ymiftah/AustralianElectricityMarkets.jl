Interconnectors and losses ​

The five NEM regions are joined by a small number of transfer paths. NEMDE does not model the lines inside a region. It models each path between two regions as one interconnector with a single flow variable, and accounts for the power lost on the way. This chapter explains the flow limits, the loss model, how losses are split between the two regions, and the special case of an interconnector that offers its capacity at a price. Symbols follow the notation table.

Each interconnector has a defined direction, from one region to the other. Its flow is positive in that direction and negative against it.

Most interconnectors are regulated: they earn a regulated revenue, offer nothing into the market, and NEMDE is free to set their flow. A market network service provider (MNSP) instead offers transfer capacity at a price, like a generator. Basslink has been the only interconnector to do so. Terranora and Murraylink carry "MNSP" in their identifiers for historical reasons and are regulated. AEMO's 2026-27 loss factor report records Basslink's notice to stop operating as an MNSP from 1 July 2026, at which point all interconnectors in the NEM would be regulated.

Flow limits ​

Each interconnector has an export limit in its defined direction and an import limit against it. The import limit is a negative number:

The slacks allow a limit to be exceeded at a penalty factor of 1150, item 5 of AEMO's Schedule of Constraint Violation Penalty Factors, v8.0. In practice the binding limits on an interconnector usually come from generic constraints, which combine its flow with the output of nearby units; see Violations, tie-breaking and prices.

Deviation in AustralianElectricityMarkets

AEMO: the loss equation is defined by integration from zero flow. The published documents do not say how the breakpoint values are placed. AustralianElectricityMarkets: when a chord spans zero flow, every breakpoint value is lowered by that chord's value at zero, so the curve passes through zero. Slopes, and therefore marginal losses, are unchanged.

Constructed coefficients. The chord from -100 to 100 MW reads 5 MW at zero flow. Subtracting 5 MW from every breakpoint value moves the curve down and keeps its slopes.

Deviation in AustralianElectricityMarketsSimulations

AEMO: the published documents do not describe how NEMDE enforces segment order. AustralianElectricityMarketsSimulations: integer variables force the segments to fill in order, which makes the problem a mixed-integer programme (SOS2 constraints). Even though the curves are convex, the possibility of negative prices makes integer variables a requirement. Prices are taken from a second solve with those integers fixed. The first and last breakpoints also bound the flow as hard limits, whatever the flow-limit slacks allow.

An interconnector that offers its capacity ​

An MNSP submits two offers, one for each direction of flow. Each has ten price bands fixed for the trading day and band quantities per interval, like an energy offer. Write for the MW cleared from band in direction , for the MW offered and for the offered availability:

The accepted bands cost   in the objective. An MNSP band clears when the price difference between the two regions is at least its offer price. The offer is, in effect, the fee for moving energy across.

The flow is measured at the receiving end. The sending end supplies the flow plus the whole loss, and each end is referred to its regional reference node by a loss factor of its own:

When the flow reverses, the sending and receiving regions swap, so the loss always falls on the region that is exporting. AEMO describes this convention in its Marginal Loss Factors for 2025-26, §5.1, p. 63.

AEMO's penalty schedule also gives an MNSP a ramp-rate limit (item 4, factor 1155), and priced violations of its total offer (item 9, factor 1135), its availability (item 16, factor 365) and its loss equations (item 17, factor 365).

Deviation in AustralianElectricityMarketsSimulations

AEMO: MNSP availability and offer limits can be violated at a penalty, the flow is ramp-limited, and a fixed loading can be offered. AustralianElectricityMarketsSimulations: models the same rows, each elastic at its penalty factor: availability, the offered band quantities, the ramp rate and the fixed loading. In addition, a binary variable allows only one direction to flow in an interval.

Deviation in AustralianElectricityMarkets

AEMO: Basslink has fixed converter losses and cannot operate in a band of low flows around zero. AustralianElectricityMarkets: neither is modelled. The share of Basslink's (T-V-MNSP1) losses assigned to its from region, Tasmania, is always set to 1.0, in place of the value AEMO publishes, which is not well defined.
