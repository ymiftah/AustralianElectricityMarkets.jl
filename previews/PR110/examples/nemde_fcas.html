FCAS capability and co-optimisation ​

Supply and demand must balance second by second, and the five-minute energy market cannot do that by itself. AEMO therefore buys reserves, called frequency control ancillary services (FCAS): capacity that is held ready to raise or lower output when frequency moves. The same generators, batteries and loads provide both energy and FCAS, and a MW held in reserve is a MW not sold as energy. NEMDE therefore clears the two together.

The ten services ​

The NEM runs at 50 Hz. A raise service increases net injection when frequency falls. A lower service decreases it when frequency rises. A generator provides raise by producing more, a load by consuming less, and a battery by either. The name describes the effect on the system, not what the provider does.

There are two families.

  • Regulation corrects the small, continuous imbalances of normal operation. Enabled units follow setpoints that AEMO's automatic generation control (AGC) sends every few seconds.

  • Contingency services are held for a sudden event, such as the loss of a large generator. They are delivered only if frequency leaves its normal band, and are distinguished by how fast they must respond.

The ten services are separate markets with separate offers and separate prices. A unit can be enabled for several at once. AEMO describes the services in its Guide to Frequency Control Ancillary Services, v4.0, §§2-3, pp. 5-8 and the delivery obligations in its Market Ancillary Service Specification.

Enablement is capacity held in reserve ​

NEMDE decides how many MW of each service to enable on each unit. Write for the MW of service enabled on unit . An enabled MW is a commitment to respond if called. It is paid for whether or not a frequency event occurs, and it adds nothing to the regional energy balance. The unit's energy target is a separate decision.

An example with a local requirement ​

Three units each have 60 MW of capability in one contingency service. A market-wide requirement needs 100 MW. A, B and C offer at $2, $5 and $9 per MW per hour, and C is the only unit in a region that could be cut off.

With the market-wide requirement alone, the cheapest allocation is 60 MW from A and 40 MW from B. Add a local requirement of 30 MW in C's region. C must now supply 30 MW, and the cheapest allocation becomes 60 MW from A, 10 MW from B and 30 MW from C. C's 30 MW counts towards both requirements, so 100 MW is enabled in total, not 130 MW.

In a real interval NEMDE can also move a unit's energy target to free up FCAS capability, or the reverse. A merit order of FCAS offers alone therefore does not reproduce the enablement. The offers, the requirements and each unit's capability are solved together (FCAS Model in NEMDE, §1.1, p. 4).

What a unit offers ​

Price bands ​

The cleared bands add up to the enablement, and is the FCAS part of the objective. An FCAS price is a price per MW enabled, per hour. AEMO's tables label it $/MWh; the MWh is a MW of capability held for an hour, not energy delivered. FCAS prices are not adjusted by loss factors.

The trapezium ​

How much FCAS a unit can provide depends on where it is operating. A generator at full output has no room to raise. A generator near its minimum has no room to lower. Each FCAS offer therefore includes an envelope, the FCAS trapezium, that gives the maximum enablement as a function of the unit's energy target. Five numbers define it.

Adjusting the offer to the interval ​

The trapezium is offered as part of a bid. Before each solve AEMO shrinks it to what the unit can do in this interval, and then checks whether the unit can be enabled at all. Neither step involves the optimisation.

Scaling ​

Three inputs can make the trapezium smaller.

  • AGC limits. A unit reports the range over which AGC can control it. A regulation trapezium is cut to that range.

  • AGC ramp rate. A unit can deliver in regulation no more than it can ramp in five minutes. A regulation trapezium is cut to that height.

  • Wind and solar forecast. A semi-scheduled unit cannot operate above its forecast (UIGF). Every one of its trapeziums is cut at that level.

AEMO: exempts fast-start units in modes 0, 1 and 2 from joint ramping. AustralianElectricityMarketsSimulations: decides the exemption from the mode the unit starts the interval in, and keeps it in both fast-start runs. A unit that starts in mode 0, 1 or 2, ends the interval in mode 3 or 4 and offers regulation therefore stays exempt in the dispatch run, where the end mode would no longer exempt it.

FCAS prices ​

The price of a service in a region is the cost of requiring one more MW of it. In the optimisation that is the sum of the marginal values of every requirement that covers the region for that service.

In the three-unit example, one more MW of the market-wide requirement comes from B at $5. One more MW of the local requirement comes from C at $9 and displaces a MW of B, a net cost of $4. Units outside the local region are paid $5 per MW per hour. C, which satisfies both requirements, is paid $5 + $4 = $9.

Two effects make real prices less direct than this.

  • Lost energy revenue. If a unit must be moved off its preferred energy target to make room for FCAS, the cost of doing so is part of the marginal cost of the service. An FCAS price can therefore exceed every FCAS offer.

  • Shortfall. If a requirement cannot be met, its marginal value is its violation penalty and not a market price. The published price is then set by a further pricing step; see Violations, tie-breaking and prices.

Only requirements set prices. The marginal value of a unit's trapezium or ramping constraint is not a service price.

Three points need care when reading these tables.

  • The limits and rates in DISPATCHLOAD are the ones dispatch used: the more restrictive of what the unit offered and what its telemetry reported. The raw telemetry is not published there.

  • RAISEREGAVAILABILITY and LOWERREGAVAILABILITY in DISPATCHLOAD are results, like actual availability. The offered amount is MAXAVAIL in BIDPEROFFER_D.

  • A requirement is defined by its coefficient rows, not by its name or its right-hand side. The rows are matched by identifier, effective date and version, and can sit in an archive month long before the interval.
